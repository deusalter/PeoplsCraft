# 6. Materials and making

This chapter says what the matter of this ground is, what each station does to it, what crystal-work is, what harms a body, and what a made thing can become. It follows the ground chapter. Section numbers beginning with 1 point there. Game things are called by key or by plain description. Heights are counted in strides up from the floor. The sea stands at 96.

Anything marked *proposed* is not in play. Chapter 8 lists all of it.

## 6.1 Hardness, and what loosens

The ground chapter gives one rule for matter. What lies over the root is set, harder the nearer it lies to the wood and the longer it has lain there. Two things follow from the rule, and making depends on both.

**Hardness.** How hard a piece is: what marks it and what it marks. Hardness comes from where the matter lay and for how long. Work does nothing to it. Iron forged ten times is still iron. Lying changes it, over generations. `wardstone` is common stone that the first comers dressed. It has lain dressed ever since, and it now needs a stone pick where raw stone needs a wooden one.

**How firmly a made thing stays in its shape.** A block in its place, a bar as a bar, an edge along its length. This starts again each time a thing is made. A sword forged this morning is as hard as its metal and has been a sword for one morning.

Making is one act at every station. The worker loosens a piece, gives it a new shape, and lets it set again. Three things loosen.

1. **A harder edge.** A tool hard enough for the piece parts it along its joints. Hands, the `workbench`, the pick, the axe and the shovel work this way.
2. **Fire.** Heat loosens the soft matter of the living ground: iron, gold, sand, clay, broken stone. `forge1` works this way.
3. **Feeder remains.** On this ground every low feeder makes set things go soft, and the raw remains of a great one go on doing it (1.6). `forge2` to `forge5`, the `tinker` bench, the gates and all crystal-work depend on this.

Nothing has to be done to make a thing set. Matter that has been loosened sets again, because it still lies over the root. A bar hardens under the hammer as metal does anywhere, and afterwards it goes on settling into its shape for as long as it lies. That is why gear out of the old stores is more often out of the ordinary than gear made this morning (6.10).

## 6.2 The ten hardnesses

A pick works on the rock a metal lies in, and that rock is always a little softer than its metal. Each metal's rock yields to a pick of the metal one step below it, or to anything harder. The metal that comes out is harder than the pick that freed it, and it cuts the next rock. No step can be skipped.

| Key | What it is | Where it lies | Its rock yields to | It is loosened by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wood` | Timber | Standing on the living ground | Hands | An edge |
| `stone` | Common rock | Everywhere under the soil | A wooden pick | An edge; fire closes rubble |
| `iron` | Iron in the upper and middle rock | Living ground, 12 to 120 | A stone pick | Fire (`forge1`) |
| `lumenite` | Lees set to a gem | Living ground, 4 to 34 | An iron pick | Nothing. It is cleaved |
| `cinder` | Iron that ran molten and set again in the deep | Living ground, 3 to 26 | A `lumenite` pick | Remains of `boss:2` (`forge2`) |
| `umbrite` | The metal of the hollow's rock | The hollow, 8 to 170 | A `cinder` pick | Remains of `boss:2` (`forge2`) |
| `noctrium` | The same metal, lower, over the body of the root | The hollow, 6 to 90 | An `umbrite` pick | Remains of `boss:4` (`forge3`) |
| `aether` | The metal of the earlier world's ground | The islands, 90 to 180 | A `noctrium` pick | Remains of `boss:4` (`forge3`) |
| `prism` | That metal with crystal set through it | The islands, 90 to 180 | An `aether` pick | Remains of `boss:6` (`forge4`) |
| `eclipse` | Iron lying on the wood, with lees set through it | Living ground, 2 to 14 | A `prism` pick | Remains of `boss:7` (`forge5`) |

**`wood`.** Timber of the living ground. It is the youngest matter there is and stands furthest from the wood. Anything with an edge takes it, and so do bare hands. It is split to planks by hand. A wooden pick is the least tool that will break stone.

**`stone`.** The common rock. It is jointed finer than a stride, so a pick of any kind brings it away as rubble (`cobble`) and bare hands bring away nothing. Fire closes rubble back to whole stone. Knapped rubble on a haft is the second tool.

**`iron`.** Ordinary iron, common from 12 to 120. It lies far from the wood and is the one hard metal a wood fire will melt. A stone pick takes its rock. Seven bars make an `anvil`, and everything harder is worked on one. Iron also comes out of old stores and out of the bodies of feeders that nested in those stores (`rustbound`). It is the last metal a crew can win without killing anything large.

**`lumenite`.** A clear gem that gives a cold light, found low in the living ground and rarely: two veins where iron has eleven. It is lees. Lees that lies in the ground with nothing to eat it sets into crystal (6.3). Close to the wood it sets further, into a gem that a hand can no longer drive and a low feeder can no longer eat. An iron pick takes its rock and the gem comes away whole. No forge is used. It is cleaved along its joints on the anvil's edge. It takes a finer edge than iron and splits under a sideways blow.

`runescribe` holds that it is crystal gone past use. `smith` holds that it is a stone, since it takes an edge and no fire touches it. One thing a player can find bears on it. The strongest of the shade-kind fed on feeders for longer than any telling reaches, and its remains are full of the gem.

**`cinder`.** The ore (`cindrite_ore`) lies in the lowest rock but one, beside the molten rock, and it is still warm when cut. The molten rock is stone the sand kinds have fed on until it runs (3.3). `cinder` is iron that ran with it and set again at that depth, all at once and close to the wood. A furnace does not move it. A `lumenite` pick takes its rock, and the black glass that forms where rock ran and set (`nightglass`) yields to the same pick for the same reason. It is loosened only in `forge2`. This is the first metal that costs a great kill, and every metal after it costs one.

**`umbrite`.** The metal of the hollow's rock, which has wood under it and wood over it. It is found at every height there. The hollow is remains of the earlier world, and nobody can say whether its metal is iron, since nobody here has seen it soft before it set. A `cinder` pick takes the ore. The remains of `boss:2` loosen it in the same bed that loosens `cinder`. What it costs is the first gate, and work in a place where feeders are up at all hours and no bed marks anything.

**`noctrium`.** The same metal in the lower half of the hollow, down to the standing water. The wood over the hollow is the shell of the root's upper face. The wood under it is the root. The lower metal has more wood near it and is set harder. An `umbrite` pick takes the ore. The remains of `boss:2` leave it as it was. The remains of the hollow's own strongest loosen it.

**`aether`.** The common metal of the islands, in sixteen veins where the living ground's iron has eleven. It is the metal of the world that grew on this root while the root lived, and it has lain there since before the living ground was. A `noctrium` pick takes the ore. `forge3` loosens it. It costs the second gate, and it is cut over a drop that does not land.

**`prism`.** Seams of the islands' metal where that world's crystal has grown through it. Old crystal is as hard as a gem and splits as a gem does. Metal bends. Set together through that whole age they do neither. An `aether` pick takes the ore. Only the remains of the islands' strongest loosen it (`forge4`). The crystal kinds of the islands feed on these seams and carry bits of the ore in their bodies.

**`eclipse`.** The lowest rock of the living ground, lying on the wood, one vein to a chunk. It is iron set as hard as matter sets here, with lees set through it. It gives a violet light, the colour of the deep kind, and arrivals say the deep kind is made of the ore. Nothing made of the living ground's own matter cuts its rock. A `prism` pick does, which is why a crew needs both gates to work its own floor. It is loosened only in `forge5`.

By this account there are three metals on the root and one gem. Iron stands at three hardnesses, the hollow's metal at two, the islands' at two, and `lumenite` is no metal. `smith` counts eight metals and sells them as eight.

## 6.3 Other matter

**Gold** lies deep (6 to 60) and stays soft. It takes almost no firmness from the wood, a wood fire melts it, and it never hardens under the hammer. It is no tier because it holds no edge. A feeder gets nothing from it, since it has nothing to lose. It is used where metal has to be bent cold and bent again, and where stone has to be faced against the stone-eating kinds (4.4).

**Coal** is old timber in the rock and new timber charred in `forge1`. **Ember dust** (`ember_dust`) is what `cinder_wisp` leaves. It burns twice as long as coal.

**Feeder remains** come in four states, and most of the trade of this ground is moving them from one state to another.

- *Raw.* Parts as they come off the body: `shade_essence`, `spore_sac`, `moth_dust`, `shard`, `rift_scale`, `moon_sliver`, `thorn`, `gel` and the rest. Raw remains are food to feeders. They go into baits and totems.
- *Boiled down.* Boiled all night in an iron pot, skimmed, and the dark residue packed in salt. This is lees: coin, and the charge of every hearth. Bone is what is left in the pot. Salted and sealed, lees lasts on this ground for generations (1.6).
- *Set.* Lees that lay where no feeder found it. Shut in rock it sets dense, as a geode (`mana_ore`), in all three places. In the open it has room and grows outward as it sets. That is the standing crystal of the caverns, the hollow and the islands. It is softer than geode crystal and it gives light, which stops feeders coming up round it (3.1). A geode gives the same light, and the rock hides it. The colour follows the kind whose lees it was: violet for the deep kind, azure for the shade-kind, pale for whatever fed on the earlier world. Close to the wood, lees sets past crystal into `lumenite`. The hollow has crystal and no gem, because it lies between two faces of the wood and on neither.
- *Great.* The raw remains of a host's strongest: `heartroot`, `scorched_core`, `warden_sigil`, `queen_spore`, `storm_plume`, `prism_heart`, `eclipse_ember`, `cairn_heart`. These go on loosening what is near them. Boiled down, the same remains would be great lees. A piece built into a forge is a piece that charges no hearth.

**Salt and pots.** Salt is dried in pans cut in the shore sandstone (3.2). Boiling down needs an iron pot, water, fuel and a night. A great carcass is boiled down in pieces. Lees is carried and held in fired clay, because salt eats iron. The game pays coin at the kill and leaves all of this out.

## 6.4 The stations

**By hand.** Splitting, knapping, tying, mixing. Four planks fitted square are a `workbench`.

**`workbench`.** A true flat face and a vice. Pieces cut to fit are joined on it. It is where timber and knapped stone become tools, where hides are cut, where a ring of eight stones is laid up round a bed, and where draughts are mixed.

**`forge1`.** A ring of rubble round a wood or coal fire. Fire loosens soft matter, and when the fire is drawn the matter sets as one piece. Iron and gold run out of their rock and set as bars. Sand sets as glass, rubble as stone, clay as brick. The same fire cooks meat and chars logs. Its cost is fuel.

**`anvil`.** A block of iron heavy enough to stand under a hammer. A bar comes out of a forge soft. Each blow firms it, and by the time the piece has its shape it is as firm as it will be that day. Two other things are done at the anvil. `lumenite` is cleaved on its edge. Frames are assembled on its face: a gate kit is hard matter fitted round great remains, and it is smith's work.

A finished piece cannot be taken back to a bar. The hammer has set it, and neither fire nor a second hammering undoes that in anything harder than iron.

**`forge2` to `forge5`.** Each is a piece of one great feeder's remains bedded in a ring of eight stones, with a fire under it. The fire wakes the remains. The remains loosen ore that no fire moves, and the metal runs together in the bed and sets as a bar.

The ring is cut from rock those remains will not eat. In each case it is rock from the animal's own ground. Whatever a great feeder could loosen where it lay, it ate long ago, and what still stands there is what it could not. The burrower's bed is ringed with the deep rock it passed through (`deepstone`), the hollow's strongest with `gloomstone`, the islands' strongest with `riftstone`, and `boss:7` with `nightglass`.

A stronger animal's remains loosen harder ore, and they loosen softer ore faster, which is why a high forge runs low work quickly. A forge still has a fire in it and still cooks. It is also a store of great remains with nobody's hand on it, and it draws.

**`tinker` bench.** `heartroot` held between two iron bars over a plank top. The remains of `boss:1` are the weakest of the eight. They move no ore. They do loosen a made thing, where the thing is small and has not been long in its shape. Two worked pieces laid against the remains go soft enough to be pressed into one, and the one sets. It will not take apart a thing that has lain long, and it will not touch a blade.

**`runeloom`.** A frame for cutting lines evenly and for laying fibre under even tension. In a ward housing the holder's hand is joined to the charge by lines cut in the stone. A staff is carried, so its lines are cut along the wood from the grip to the head, and the frame holds the stave true while they are cut. Fibre laid round crystal (`weave`) is the same path made up as cloth. A badly cut line leaks what the hand drives into the arm that drives it. Everything a hand drives is made here: staves, totems, the lined cloth and the bone-set harness worn by people who drive, crystal trinkets, the `recall_stone`, and the bait for `boss:7`.

**`chest`.** A box. A chest of ordinary goods draws nothing. A chest with great remains in it is a store, and it draws.

**Gate frames.** A gate has no fire. Remains that nothing wakes do little: enough to hold an opening, too little to loosen the frame. So a frame needs only to be hard, and the two kits use what a crew has when it reaches them.

## 6.5 Why each first killing opens the next

The hardness order and the order of the eight are one chain. Each link is a rock a crew cannot cut, a metal it cannot loosen, or a place it cannot reach.

| First kill | What its remains do | What that opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `boss:1` | Loosen small made things | The `tinker` bench |
| `boss:2` | Loosen iron that fire leaves hard | `cinder`, and later `umbrite`. A `cinder` pick is what takes `umbrite` ore |
| `boss:3` | Hold a relation to the hollow | The first gate. `umbrite`, then the `umbrite` pick that takes `noctrium` ore |
| `boss:4` | Loosen the lower metal of the hollow | `noctrium`, whose pick takes `aether` ore. Later `aether` itself |
| `boss:5` | Hold a relation to the islands | The second gate. `aether`, then the pick that takes `prism` ore |
| `boss:6` | Loosen metal with crystal through it | `prism`, whose pick takes `eclipse` ore |
| `boss:7` | Loosen iron that lies on the wood | `eclipse`, and the bait for the last |
| `boss:8` | Nothing is built from it yet | The strongest charge there is |

**What great remains are spent on.** A first kill yields six to nine pieces. Every use wants a piece of its own: one in each forge, one in each bench, one in each gate, and, once stones can be held, one boiled down for each night a great stone is held. These uses exclude each other. A crew that builds three forges and two gates from one animal has that much less to boil down. Each built piece also stands unheld and draws. So every metal above iron costs a kill, and what the kill lets loose.

## 6.6 Crystal-work

The game's magic is one craft. It has three parts and all three are in the ground chapter: a cut line, a piece of feeder remains, and a hand that drives.

**Driving.** A holder drives a charge with a hand on the stone and pays for it in the body. Anything else that is driven costs the same way. What the body does is known. The hand closes on the worn place and the arm is held hard, as if pushing at a weight that does not move. What comes back is strain up the arm. A tired arm shakes, then stops answering, and the working stops with it. The blue bar is how much more the arm will take. What passes along the line from the hand to the remains is not known to anyone on the ground, on this world or the ones arrivals came from, and the trade has never needed to know.

**Lines.** A path from the hand to the remains. Cut in hard stone it lasts as long as the stone. Cut in wood or laid in fibre it can be carried. A good line loses little. A bad one leaks into the arm, and the bar empties faster for the same work. Where to cut is learned by the hand and cannot be told.

**Crystal** (`mana_crystal`). Lees set hard. Driven, it does the one thing feeder remains do: it loosens what it is pointed at. It does nothing else.

**A staff** is three sticks, a crystal and two pieces of metal, with lines cut along the wood. One piece of metal is the seat the crystal sits against. The crystal throws off its seat. A soft seat soaks up part of every throw, so a staff throws as hard as its seat is hard, and each staff is made of the tier whose metal it carries. From `aether` upward the seat is hard enough that a throw is not spent on the first body it meets. A harder throw costs the arm more.

**A totem** is two carved bones, a billet of metal and two sticks. Each bone has raw remains of the shade-kind bound to it (`effigy`). Raw remains are food. The lowest feeders have no shape of their own and form round whatever they are feeding on. One forms on the bones. Then it takes its body from the nearest hard matter, which is the billet. So the thing that comes off a totem is as hard as the totem's metal, and its look follows the metal (7.5).

The bearer drives the totem once, as the food is opened. What forms there forms under a driven hand and takes that hand for a stronger animal whose food this is. It stays beside the bearer on sufferance and goes for other feeders, which are food to it. That one drive is the twenty the bar shows. A bearer can have one such animal under the hand, and a few worn pieces let the hand have another. When the bearer goes down nothing is driving and the animal is gone.

**The `recall_stone`** is the nearest thing here to a ward housing small enough to carry: a stone with cut lines, two crystals in place of a charge, and a `lumenite` gem where a core would sit. The gem is the one part of it that loosening does not move, and the working turns on it and into the hand that drives. The bearer is loosened for a moment and the way takes them (1.6). The stone is warm afterwards because it has loosened itself a little too, and it will not work again until it has set.

**Tonics** (`tonic`, `greater_tonic`). Ground crystal in fruit juice. An arm that has driven too much shakes because it is clenched. Crystal taken in small amounts eases it, and it answers again. After a tonic the arm is as spent as it was and has stopped saying so. The game shows the bar refilled and no later cost. The cost that follows from the cause is in 6.8.

**Limits.** These hold for every piece of crystal-work now in the game and for any added later.

- One hand drives one working. A second person adds nothing.
- Nothing is done by word, sign or gathering. A working is a line, remains and a hand.
- Crystal loosens. Raw remains feed. Driven lees turns away what is weaker than the kill in it. No fourth effect exists.
- A working is as strong as its hardest part and as costly as its worst line.
- Remains nobody is driving draw feeders.

## 6.7 Trinkets and arrows

A trinket is a small worked piece. Each does one thing, and a second of the same adds nothing, because the one thing is already done. They fall into five sorts by what is in them.

| Sort | What is in it | Which |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain good gear | Leather, iron, glass, cut to fit a body | The boots and anklet that help the legs. The iron guard for the arm. The thorned guard, which is set with `thorn` so that what strikes it is cut. The ground glass that helps an archer's eye. The long glove that adds reach. The fins |
| Crystal-work | Crystal on a short line, worn where the driving arm can use it | The ring and the charm that ease a driving arm, and the larger piece that does both. `prism_core` (4.8) |
| Raw remains | A piece of a feeder, unsalted | The whistle packed with remains, and `mycelial_crown` (4.6), on each of which one more small feeder forms. The delver's piece, a scale of the stone-eating kind worn at the wrist, which loosens rock a little under the pick. The dark cloak, woven with the stuff of the shade-kind, which is hard to see at its edges. `mossheart_sprig`, `maw_fang`, `wardens_aegis`, `eclipse_shard` (chapter 4) |
| The pale stuff of the islands | A wad of the ground that hangs under the islands (3.5) | The bottle, the feather charm, the emblem and the treads that give a second step in the air or a soft landing. `tempest_feather` (4.7) |
| No cause | A number and nothing else | The locket, the two bands that add to a blow, the coin that brings more from a kill, the shell that draws loose things in, `sovereign_light`. These belong to the game (1.9) |

**The second step in the air.** Ground here stays where it is put, and the pale stuff of the islands is ground that is nearly gone: it bears weight for a moment and weighs almost nothing. A wad of it let out under the foot is a piece of ground in the air for one step. The same stuff under the heel takes the force out of a landing. The first comers brought it home through their gates, which is why such pieces lie in old stores on the living ground.

**Arrows.** A bow throws an edge, and an edge does what its hardness lets it (6.8). The five arrowheads are five hardnesses: flint, barbed iron, `cinder`, `umbrite` and `prism`. A `cinder` head comes off the anvil holding the heat of the deep, and it burns what it enters.

## 6.8 What harms a body

Six things harm a body here, and each follows from what things are made of. The game has one number for harm today. What follows is the ground for damage kinds and states if the game adds them, and all of it is *proposed* except where a line says it is in play.

**An edge.** Swords, axes, arrowheads, teeth, thorn. Flesh, hide, timber and fungus yield to it. Anything harder than the edge turns it, and loose grit closes behind it.

**Weight.** Picks, shovels, clubs, a great feeder's bulk. Brittle things break under it: rust, crystal, stone, gem, glass. Things that give do not: green wood, fungus, jelly, cloth.

**Fire.** Flame, molten rock, the kinds that are bodied in heat. Timber, thorn, fibre, hide, fungus and raw remains burn. Stone, grit, metal and anything that came out of the molten deep do not. Fire destroys remains: a kill finished by fire has less to boil down and leaves less that draws. The sand-country people burn every kill for that reason. A later system can pay less coin for a burned kill.

**Loosening.** A driven crystal, the touch of a feeder as it feeds, great remains handled bare. Whatever is set yields to it: plate, stone, crystal, the bodies of feeders built of hard matter. Things with nothing to lose do not: gold, grit, jelly, green wood, the shade-kind. Neither do things past loosening: `lumenite`, bone. A body or a plate that has been loosened counts for less for a few moments, and then sets again, as everything here does. So loosening is strongest against the best armour, and a blow that follows it goes in.

**A fall.** In play. A drop onto set ground harms any body, and nothing worn stops it, since the armour stops and the body inside it does not. The pale stuff of the islands is the one thing that helps (6.7).

**The sun.** In play. Full daylight undoes the two kinds that took their bodies where there is no sun. No lamp, fire or made thing does the same.

**The tool that takes a kind of ground whole takes apart a body made of that ground.** Low feeders are bodied in what they feed on. An axe goes through the thorn and sprout kinds as it goes through timber. A pick breaks the kinds bodied in stone, rust and crystal. A shovel scatters the grit kinds, and a sword does little to them. This is the rule the game already applies to blocks, carried to creatures.

Four states of a body follow from the same causes.

**Spore in a wound.** The hollow's kinds are bodied in spore and fungus and leave some of it in a cut. It grows in the dark and takes strength steadily. Daylight ends it, and so does fire held to the wound, or a salve made with the fungus itself (`greater_salve`). The hollow has no daylight, so a crew that stays below carries it until someone treats it.

**An overdriven arm.** Driving past what the arm will take, which a tonic makes possible. The palm is raw, the hand loses feeling, and every working costs more until a night's sleep mends it. Holders who drove that way for half a lifetime have a hand that never came back. This is the cost of the tonic that the game leaves out.

**Carrying raw great remains.** A person carrying them, or a spent charge, draws every feeder in reach, as a spent stone does. It ends when the remains are built into something with a ring or a frame round them, boiled down and salted, or put down. A crew walking home from a first kill is in this state the whole way.

**Turned.** A feeder that meets a driven charge with a stronger kill in it than itself will not cross the edge. It stands off and waits for the hand to tire. This falls on feeders only, and only a householder can cause it.

## 6.9 Food, hunger and the bed

**Hunger.** A body that cuts stone and fights uses itself up. The hunger bar is how long it can go on. Running, being struck, striking, breaking ground and healing all empty it. A fed body mends itself. A starved one wastes, and at the end of its strength the way takes it like any other failing body.

**What there is to eat.** Meat comes from the ordinary animals, hide from the grazer and the rooter, and feather from the bird (7.2). Some feeders also leave hide, which is the hide of what they ran down. Fruit comes from trees and bushes. Mushrooms grow in the shade and underground. Nothing is tilled (1.6). Fire trebles what meat is worth. Food worth a full meal leaves a body mending faster for a while (`fed`).

**Salves** (`salve`) are fruit, mushroom and fibre bound on a wound. They close it. A body can take only so much of that at once, and the game refuses a second salve until the first has done its work. **`ironbark`** and **`swiftroot`** are a bark draught that toughens skin for a few minutes and a flower tea that quickens the legs. All three are ordinary herb-work.

**Food of the hollow.** The lit fungus of the hollow grows on rock full of geodes and takes up crystal. Eaten as broth (`glow_broth`) it feeds, and it eases the arm as a weak tonic does. The brown caps of the hollow make a plain stew.

**Food of the islands.** `skyfruit` is the fruit that hung on the island trees at that world's last dusk. Where the hour stands it neither ripens nor rots, on the tree or in a store. It feeds and it closes wounds a little. In play it comes out of the first comers' stores on the islands, and `provisioner` sells it once a crew has crossed, having bought it from crews as they come back. No tree gives it in play. *Proposed:* the lit leaves drop one now and then, and none grows back.

**The bed.** A `bedroll` is fibre over boards. Nothing in it does any work. The lying down is what moves the near end of a way (1.6). A `guest_bed` is hide over boards, because a holder sits up on it every night and fibre does not last under that.

**Sleep.** A body that slept a night through mends faster and moves easier for a short time after waking (`dawn`).

**On rising.** A body the way has carried comes out hurt, half fed, with the arm half spent and every draught gone from it. Draughts wear off in the wood. Hunger does not.

## 6.10 Modifiers and rarities

A modifier is a fact about how one piece set. Made pieces carry one about a third of the time. Things cut out of a feeder's body carry one more often, because the body was built of whatever the animal ate. Things from deep stores carry one most often, because they have lain longest.

| Key | On | What it is a fact about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `keen` | Weapon | The edge closed to a single line with no flaw along it |
| `savage` | Weapon | The metal passed through a feeder, or was quenched in lees. It loosens a little where it cuts |
| `swift` | Weapon | It set with less metal in it than its shape suggests |
| `heavy` | Weapon | It set dense. More metal than its size, and slower in the hand |
| `dull` | Weapon | The bar had half set before the last blows. The edge never closed |
| `blessed` | Weapon | It set all at once, in one working with no pause, and has no soft place in it. Smiths cannot do this on purpose. The born call it favour |
| `warding` | Armour | It set thickest where blows land |
| `stalwart` | Armour | It set dense throughout. It turns more and weighs more |
| `brittle` | Armour | It was hammered after it had set, and has cracks too fine to see |
| `arcane` | Any | Its grain runs one way from hand to head, and a line cut along it leaks less |
| `radiant` | Any | Old lees has set into the metal, as in `eclipse` ore. It shows a faint cold light |
| `menacing` | Trinket | It has a grain of a strong kill's lees in it, and loosens a little where the bearer strikes |
| `vital`, `fleet`, `lucky` | Any | The game's labels. No cause is offered |

A rarity is a fact about how deep a thing's matter lay, and for made things how long the thing has lain made. The seven grades are one scale. The colours have no cause.

| Rarity | Grade | What it is a fact about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Common | Surface matter and green work: timber, rubble, hide, fibre |
| 1 | Uncommon | Matter of the upper rock, worked by fire: iron |
| 2 | Rare | Matter of the deep rock of the living ground: `lumenite`, `cinder` |
| 3 | Epic | Matter from between two faces of the wood: the hollow |
| 4 | Legendary | Matter of the earlier world: the islands |
| 5 | Mythic | Matter that lay on the wood: `eclipse` |
| 6 | Relic | A made thing that has lain made since the first comers. Only what comes out of the base of the eighth stone is of this grade today |

A modifier moves a piece one or two grades because a piece that set well is worth what deeper matter is worth, and a piece that set badly is worth less than its metal.

## 6.11 Old things a crew can find

Each of these is *proposed* as a kind of find for the game to place. Each shows a cause that a player could not otherwise see, or does something in play that follows from one. None is a single object: the ground is wide, and what was made once here was made many times.

**A cutter's saw.** A two-handled saw of steel from another world, made for cutting broken root-wood, lying at the bottom of a shaft sunk to the floor with its middle teeth stripped. The teeth at either end are whole, and it goes through standing timber faster than any axe made here. It shows what the first comers came for and what the floor is.

**A core under a hearth.** A ward core cut on another world: black, cold, and as heavy as a block of stone many times its size. Some first comers built house wards the way they are built everywhere else, with wood under the hearth, before they knew this ground needed none. A few are still under the hearthstones of old houses. No tool marks one and no fire touches it. Set down beside the floor it is the same stuff, and that is where an arrival can learn what the floor is. It also shows that the builders came from somewhere wards need cores.

**A harness made for a born hunter.** `lumenite` scale riveted over `cinder` plate, heavier than anything an arrival wore, because its wearer could not fall and rise. It turns more than any harness of its metals and slows whoever wears it. It comes out of the remains of a great feeder, which is where its wearer went (4.2).

**A sealed jar of great lees.** A clay jar from the first comers' time, salted and sealed with pitch. It is the charge of some host's strongest, killed when the stones were held. Set in a great stone and driven, it would hold that stone for one night with no new kill. Opened anywhere else it draws at once. It lies in a `dungeon` behind a wall with no door.

**A crystal in the shape of a pot.** A single crystal with the form of the inside of a jar. It was a store where the salt failed and nothing found the lees, and the clay has been broken off it. It is enough crystal for a staff head or a long run of tonics, and it is where a player can see what crystal is.

**A staff of stone.** A rod of dressed stone with cut lines, and a crystal at the head on a `lumenite` seat. It dates from before anyone here cut lines in wood. It is heavy and slow, it throws harder than any staff of its age, and it costs the arm little, because a line cut in hard stone does not leak. It lies in a vault in the hollow.

**A net for fliers.** A throwing net of `aether` wire with weights at the rim, made for the kind that never lands. A flier caught in it comes down and stays down for a short time. It lies in a store on the islands.

**A bench with stronger remains in it.** A bench built round the remains of the hollow's strongest. It loosens blades and plate, which the `tinker` bench cannot (6.12). It is sealed in a room of a `dungeon`, and since it is a store of great remains, feeders are thick in the rooms round it.

## 6.12 Making a made thing better

Work changes how well a piece sits in its shape and leaves hardness alone. An iron sword stays iron under any hammer. What improves is how true the edge lies, how the weight sits, how little the line leaks. The game has one way to do it. Two more follow from the same causes and are *proposed*.

**Join.** In play. Two small pieces are loosened on the `tinker` bench and pressed into one. The new piece does what both did. Cost: both pieces, and one piece of `heartroot` in the bench.

**Rework.** A made piece is laid against great remains until it goes soft, then worked again under the hammer and left to set. It is the same metal and may be a better or a worse piece, since no two workings fall alike. The remains of `boss:1` are too weak to loosen a blade. A bench built round a stronger animal's remains would do it, up to the hardness that animal's forge can loosen. Cost: a piece of great remains for the bench, a bar of the same metal, fuel, and the piece itself at hazard. Such a bench draws harder than a forge does, because it has no fire and no ring.

**Leave it to lie.** A piece left alone in its shape settles, faster the nearer it lies to the wood. Gear left in a chest on the floor of the living ground, or in the hollow, is a little better each time the moon comes round. Cost: the use of the piece for that time, and the walk.

Great remains pay for the first two, and they are also the only thing that charges a great stone.

## 6.13 Open

- The great stones cannot be broken. Long lying explains part of that (6.1) and no more. How the first comers cut them is not settled.
- The looks of the nine things that form on totems follow the metal. No closer cause is offered for any one of them (7.5).
- Salt, pots and the night over the fire are left out of play. Nothing here needs them added, and a pan at the shore would show where salt comes from (3.2).
