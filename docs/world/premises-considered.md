# Premises considered

Five founding premises were written from five starting points, attacked for sameness with other games and for unfaithfulness to what they borrowed, and revised once. After revision all five stood on the same two borrowed rules: how a ward is built and fails, and how a host of feeders is led. They were one premise seen from five sides. The ground chapter takes one as its spine and grafts from the other four.

## The five

**From the mechanics: lost cutters and lees.** An ordinary world lost its road, and ward-cutters from other worlds get lost in the dead layers beside it and come out with nothing. They live by killing feeders and boiling them down, and the remains are money, bait and the thing that softens old metal. Remains with a hand on them turn feeders and remains with no hand draw them, which accounts for raids, totems and the rising difficulty.

**From the cosmos: wards on a dead root.** The ground is a small world lying on the dead root of an earlier one, so the floor is ward-wood, set stone stays where it is put, and a room can be held with a charge and a hand. Arrivals came down a dead road alone and are carried back along it when they are beaten down, while people born here die. The title word is a second plural, for persons who are many and are no people.

**From the title: steady ground and a rendering frontier.** The same dead wood under the ground, with a company of builders who came for cores, could not cut the wood, and built killing floors to render strong beasts instead. Their mark, one letter short, is cut on every old stone and was taken for the name of the place. A late arrival works the floors again while marchers born here dispute whose they are.

**From the bosses: the uneaten kill.** The old stones are spent wards whose wood still holds, and the strongest animal of each host lies under the stones that smell of its kind. A crew baits it with a charge nobody is driving, kills it and renders it uneaten, so its host breaks into packs. The title names what people do with a kill, and a fall is only a fall: the crew carries the fallen home.

**From the ruins: the unhunted ground.** The great stones are stands built for getting the remains of a host's strongest, because a place is only as safe as the largest thing its people have killed. Nobody has hunted at them in living memory, so the great ones have grown far past what a hunted host would carry and the villages have emptied. Outsiders are brought in to hunt again, and an army comes to claim what they kill.

## Chosen

The premise from the cosmos. The dead root under the ground is one cause for the most mechanics, including the awkward ones: blocks that hang in the air, a floor that cannot be broken, a house that is safe with no ward core, a return at the bed that also works from the two other realms, and a stone that does the same thing on purpose. It was the only premise that gave the return a cause inside the world without ending death.

One part of it was changed. Its title word was a plural invented to describe the arrivals. It is now the word the born use for them, because the born can see that arrivals get up after a killing and they do not. That gives the word a speaker, a reason and a consequence in play: the born hold the hearths and the arrivals hunt.

## Grafted

- **From the bosses.** The engine. Bait is a charge nobody drives, laid on a spent stone, and the strongest comes to eat. This predicts the co-op rule that the fight goes on while anyone who fought stays near. The chosen premise's own version, a single holder with a hand on the stone, predicted the opposite. Also taken: crystal as old lees, and the blue bar as the strain on an arm.
- **From the mechanics.** The arrivals as core-cutting crews lost in the dead wood, which accounts for several players, late arrival and empty hands at once. Feeders eating the set out of things, as the reason their remains work a forge, a bench and a gate.
- **From the title.** The bed as the near end of a way that stays open in dead wood. Hardness by nearness to the wood. The sealed stores, and a people who burn what they kill.
- **From the ruins.** Low feeders bodied in what they feed on, so that drops are diet. The great ones grown large because nobody hunted them. The Guide as an order of work that leaves out its costs.

## Why the others lost

- **The mechanics.** It had no cause for the return at the bed and said so. Its title was a copying error on a stone, which gave the spelling a cause and no meaning.
- **The title.** To stay clear of the borrowed creatures it made the hostile things a local kind, which cut the ward loose from what a ward is made of. Its builders were a company, an invented body filling a gap. Its title was a mark with a letter missing, which had a cause and changed nothing in play.
- **The bosses.** It refused any cause for the return and had the crew carry the fallen, which fails for a player alone, for lava and for a fall off the islands. Its forges needed ground with no wood near, so it could not share the root that explains the rest. Its title's spelling was a word run together in the mouth.
- **The ruins.** The fallen lie cold and walk home, which is no cause either. Its title was a worn spoken form, which is spelling by ear. Its army came from outside with guides of its own, which raised more questions about roads than it answered.

## What is still weak

- The way through dead wood is a new rule. It never fails in play, so nothing shows its limits.
- Five of the eight first kills show only the general hardening.
- No ward is held by a player yet.
- The round of eight nights and the night of the rising are given as facts, without causes.
- Every hostile creature is a low feeder of the borrowed kind, with bodies and a weakness to sunlight that the source does not give them.
