# Borrowed

The game's world stands inside the cosmos of an existing work of fiction. This file lists every element taken from that work's world. Only the frame is taken: how the cosmos works, the kinds of thing that exist, and the terms for them. No person, place, people or event of the source appears in the game, and the game is set on ground the source does not describe.

Borrowed elements may not be changed. Each row gives the element, how it stands in the source in this file's own words, how the game uses it, and what the game adds that the source does not say. Every addition is also a row in `invented.md`.

**Standing** says how firmly the game may lean on a row.

- *settled*: the source states it in a fixed form.
- *reference*: it is in the source's reference material.
- *provisional*: it rests on working material or on a single passage. The game leans on these least, and they are the first to change if the source changes.

| Worlds are whole, never planets | Each world is a complete reality with its own ground, sky, sun and stars. Nothing lies between worlds and nothing lies past a world's sky. ## Wood and wards

| Element | How it stands in the source | How the game uses it | What the game adds | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dead root-wood | Wood from the dead roots of earlier worlds. Black, heavy, cold, proof against fire and rot. It holds ground steady against the Fraying. Crews cut it in dead and dangerous places, and it is rare and fought over | The floor of the game's world is a whole dead root | A root that is sound and so cannot be cut. A whole world lying on one. Ground that parts in even pieces and stays where it is put. Matter set harder near the wood | settled |
| Old wood holds longer, and wood wears | Older, deeper wood holds harder and for longer. A ward on a very old core can stand a long time with no hand on it. A core that has held long enough is spent and falls apart | Stated in 1.2. Whether this root is wearing is left unknown | A cause why no ward on this ground stands without a hand, though the wood under it is the oldest there is: the root is uncut and joined to no pot | settled |
| The ward | Human-made, on every world. A carved stone housing, a core of dead root-wood, a charge of boiled-down feeder remains in salt, and one trained holder with a hand on the stone who pays for it in the body | The model for every working in the game. Townsfolk rooms are house wards. The eight altars are great housings | Wards with no core, because the ground already lies on wood. Great housings cut to carry the charge of a host's strongest | settled |
| The housing | Hard stone, since soft stone loses its cut lines. A hollow worn for the hand. Lines cut from that hollow to the core and the charge. A badly cut line leaks into the holder's arm. Good cutting cannot be taught | `runescribe`'s trade. The `runeloom`, staves and the `recall_stone`. The first comers' quarries in the high country | Lines cut in wood and laid in fibre. A staff as the same work pointed outward | settled |
| No god gave wards | People developed them. Some faiths credit a god, and the trade does not | The born and the arrivals both treat ward-work as people's work | Nothing | settled |
| What a charge turns | A ward turns away anything weaker than the strongest kill boiled into its charge | Why great lees is wanted, why a host's strongest is hunted, and why a pack led by something stronger crosses a house ward during a raid | The reading of altar spacing as reach | settled |
| The life of a charge | A charge lasts until first light. Spent or undriven, it is carrion and draws feeders. A held ward draws feeders to its edge | Bait at an altar is an undriven charge. Stores draw raids. Two held hearths draw packs. Nobody holds where there is no dawn | Bait vessels. Raids. The refusal of townsfolk to settle in the two other places | settled |
| Wood without charge | Wood alone steadies ground and stops no feeder | The standing condition of the whole world | The extension from a road post to a world | settled |
| What crosses a ward | People, weapons, arrows and fire cross freely. So do feeders that never land and feeders that pass deep underground | Town protection ends when armed people march. The bait for the flier is flown and the bait for the burrower is sunk. In sand country a ward only drew | The march of the sand-country people, and the reason their forebears gave up the pot | settled |
| The holder's cost | Holding strains the arm and the body. Driving a ward past its limit can deaden the hand or kill the holder. The holder must feel where the edge lies | The blue bar. Old holders' hands. Why no arrival holds | An arrival at the end of their strength is taken by their way and leaves the stone | settled |
| The house ward | Root-wood under a hearthstone and a pot of lees, held on bad nights by the householder with a hand on the hearth | Every occupied room in the game | The wood is left out, since the floor supplies it. The rules for a room: closed, lit, with a door, of a size one hand can hold | settled |
| One holder; ceremony does nothing | Holding is one person's work. Nothing is done by many voices together, and no ceremony, wording or gesture has any effect anywhere | A rule for all game text and all future systems. Offerings work by their material | Nothing | settled |
| Lees | The dark residue left when feeder carcasses are boiled down in iron pots, packed in salt. Unsalted, it is food to feeders. How long it lasts, salted or not, is unsettled in the source, and the figures it gives are short | The game's coin. Totems carry unsalted remains. Nests are stores where the salt failed | Lees as money. Sealed lees lasting generations on this ground, and uneaten lees setting to crystal. Both are the game's own, with their own cause, and do not rest on the source's unsettled point | settled for what lees is; the game's additions are marked stretch in `invented.md` |
| Cutting crews | Crews go into the dead remains of earlier worlds behind a pathfinder to cut root-wood. Few cutters live through many trips, and pathfinding shortens a life | The players are such crews, lost. The first way was held open by one pathfinder after another | A root they cannot cut. One mouth | settled for the crews; reference for the pathfinder |

## Feeders

| Element | How it stands in the source | How the game uses it | What the game adds | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rel'shari | The things that feed. The plain word in the source is feeders. Feeding is what they are | Every hostile creature except the human fighters. Game text says feeders | Their bodies on this ground, and the undoing of two kinds by sunlight | reference |
| Kṣudhita and Jāgrat | The two low ranks. The first has no lasting shape and forms round each hunger, and made things near it go soft. The second holds a body with a shape that belongs to its kind, and has no human form. Neither has a self or speech. A trained fighter can kill one | The game's creatures and its eight great feeders | Every low feeder on this ground softens set things. Great remains go on eating the firmness out of things: forges, the bench, gates. Low feeders bodied in what they feed on. Three kinds drawn upright by the game, which the bible forbids to be likenesses of people | provisional |
| No higher feeder here | Feeders with a self are as strong as demigods, and no mortal beats one by force | None exists on this ground and none may be written | Nothing | settled |
| Might is the only order among feeders | They have no organisations of any kind. A host follows its strongest | The eight great feeders lead hosts and command nothing | Many hosts of each of eight kinds | settled |
| Succession by eating | Whoever kills and eats the strongest is followed in its place | The cause of the difficulty step after each first kill, and of the three events | Lesser animals coming in to a failing strongest | settled |
| A host breaks when its strongest dies uneaten | If the strongest is killed by a mortal and left uneaten, the host breaks into packs and goes everywhere at once | The same | Packs as food for other kinds, so that everything hostile hardens | reference |
| Ground and rank among feeders | The weaker stay off a stronger one's ground and eat where it allows | Why few creatures come near a live boss, why no raid falls on the dark night, why each first kill opens ground | The deep kind that `boss:3` fed on | reference |
| Feeders arise within the roots | The low ranks arise inside the Tree's roots and are found on the roads there | Feeders come up out of the root | In any unlit place on this ground | reference |
| A lone walker passes | On the roads in the roots, a single traveller is beneath the notice of what lives there and a large party is taken | Why arrivals come up one at a time | Applied to one dead road. A load draws as a company does, so they come up with nothing | reference |
| Light and feeders | A lamp, and people awake, hold low feeders off. Nobody sees one cross open ground | The light rule for spawning. Nobody sees a creature arrive | Light stops them coming up and does not turn one that is up. Daylight counted as light. The watch of waking people is left out, since play does not show it | provisional |
