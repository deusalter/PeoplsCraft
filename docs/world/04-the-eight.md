# 4. The eight that are called

This chapter covers the eight great feeders, `boss:1` to `boss:8`. For each it says what the animal is, why its bait reaches it, what each part of the fight is, what its killing floor shows, what the kill yields, and what the first kill costs every player on the server. It follows the ground chapter. Section numbers beginning with 1 point there.

Game things are called by key or by plain description. Names, titles and lines now in the game are gathered in 4.13 with what each has to stop claiming.

## 4.1 What all eight are

Each of the eight is a Jāgrat: a low feeder that holds a body. None has a self. None speaks, bargains or waits for anyone. Nobody set any of them anywhere.

Each is the strongest animal of a host. A spent great stone smells of the kind last boiled into it, and feeders are drawn to remains of their own kind, so the strongest of each host lies at the stones of its own smell. It has fed there since the born stopped hunting. It is large because nothing has killed it. Its size is bulk.

There are eight kinds of host on this ground, and many hosts of each kind, one to a stretch of country (1.6). The first comers cut a kind of great housing for each kind they found. When a crew kills the strongest of a host, the next animal to answer bait at stones of that kind is the strongest of a neighbouring host, moved onto the empty ground. It has had the same long time unhunted and is as large. So great remains can be had for as long as a crew will pay a bait and a fight.

The eight kinds have no order among themselves except strength. A weaker one stays off a stronger one's ground, and that is all. The numbering from 1 to 8 is the order of strength as the first comers found it by killing. The spacing of the six kinds of stone on the living ground still shows it. The Guide follows that order because each kill yields what the next kill needs. The eighth kind leads none of the other seven. It is followed only when a pack has nothing else over it and it is the strongest thing present.

Chapter 7 says which lesser kinds run with which.

## 4.2 What a calling is

**Bait.** A bait is remains and a vessel. The remains are the raw, unsalted remains of the host's lesser animals. Laid on a spent stone they are a small carcass of the animal's own kind, lying where its kind have died. The lesser animals leave it to the strongest.

The vessel has two jobs. It puts the remains where this animal feeds: in sand, in the air, in smoke, on bare stone. And it holds them. Raw remains go on loosening whatever touches them, weakly, and the remains of a stronger host do it faster. A vessel softer than its remains need is eaten through. A vessel of the right matter holds them for as long as it lies sealed. The first three baits are held by wood, stone and iron. From the fourth on, each bait is made in the metal the kill before it opened, because nothing softer holds those remains.

Nothing else about a bait does anything. The carving of the first, the tone of the third and the look of the seventh do nothing. The game's verbs for the eight baits (wake, call up, ring, burn, fly, focus, wear, open) are the born's account of what the crew is doing, and belong only in the born's mouths. In play every bait is laid on its stone and is gone.

**What lies ready.** Four baits are found in old stores: `verdant_idol`, `warden_bell`, `spore_censer` and `prism_lens`. Each is remains sealed in matter that lasts. No `sunken_lure` is found, because it holds meat. No `storm_kite` is found, because cloth and feather do not last. No `eclipse_mask` and no `cairn_key` is found. Both carry slivers of the deep kind bound on the outside of the piece, and those are the strongest low remains there are. In a store with a nest in it they were the first thing eaten.

**The fight.** The animal comes up to eat, and the bait is gone when it arrives. What the game shows as a fight is a crew killing an animal at its food. The stages of the fight are a hurt animal changing what it does.

**What it throws.** A feeder is bodied in what it feeds on. Everything one of the eight throws is a piece of its body: root, grit, pale matter, cap, crystal, sliver, lit stone. A fan is what comes off when it turns on one hunter. A ring is what comes off when it rears or lands. A spiral is what comes off while it turns. Strikes on marked ground come from an animal that is overhead, or from something of its body that is.

**Changing place.** `boss:3`, `boss:6`, `boss:7` and `boss:8` are in one place and then in another. Every feeder is found where it was not (1.6). These four do it in front of a crew, because nothing on the floor is strong enough to make them wary.

**Hanging in the air.** `boss:3`, `boss:6` and `boss:8` hang and do not walk. That belongs to the game (1.9). `boss:4` hangs for a plain reason: it is fastened to the roof. `boss:5` flies.

**The lesser ones that come.** At a set point in each fight lesser animals of the host arrive. Among feeders a kill is eaten, and one that eats the strongest is followed in its place. A strongest that is failing is about to be food. The lesser ones that had fed near it on sufferance come in for it, and the crew is standing in the way. The game never shows one of them eating it. The crew kills the animal and boils it down, nothing is followed, and the host breaks.

**The metal in it.** Each of the eight drops worked bars. A great animal eats each bait vessel and all, and it lies on a floor where born hunters failed (2.4). What they carried is in it. The weakest kind was hunted longest by the born, and it is full of their iron.

**The crystal on the floors.** Crystal is old lees set hard. The crystal that caps the pillars at `arena3` and `arena7`, and stands as pillars at `arena6`, is lees. The first comers boiled each carcass down on the floor where it fell, and what spilled has set.

## 4.3 `boss:1`: the strongest of the woodland host

**In the game.** `b_colossus`, at `altar1` in `arena1`. It walks, and its footfalls are heard. From the start it slams the ground for six strides round it and it charges. At half strength four `sproutling` come. After that: a wider slam with a ring of ten root spikes, a charge, seven root spikes on marked ground, and two more `sproutling`. It drops `heartroot`, sometimes `mossheart_sprig`, salves and iron bars. The floor is a clearing of mossy stone with six living oaks standing as pillars, glowing fungus, and the stone on a platform of mossy brick. Stones of this kind lie the closest together of the six on the living ground, and one lies a short walk from the mouth.

**What it is.** The strongest of the thorn and sprout kinds, which feed on green growth. It is bodied in root, bark and the moss that covers anything that lies still in a wood. When it comes up at a stone its roots go down into the joints of the paving and up the six oaks.

**The bait.** `verdant_idol`: a log, plant fibre, two pieces of `gel` and a red flower. `gel` is the raw remains of `gelcap`, a lesser animal of this host. The log is the vessel, and to an eater of growth it is also food, so the animal takes vessel and remains together. The fibre binds it. Householders say the log must be carved as a likeness, and they put a red flower on it. An uncarved log holding the same remains would bring the same animal. The flower does nothing. It is on the pattern because the first offerings laid at these stones in the quiet generations had one (5.2).

**The fight.** The slam is its weight and the charge is an animal's rush. At half strength it pulls itself off the floor it has rooted into. The roots under the paving come up in a ring round it, and the lengths that had grown up the oaks come down from the crowns. The `sproutling` are the smallest of its host.

**The floor.** The paving is mossy because moss and root are what hold it together now. The first comers left the oaks standing as the posts of the floor. They are old because nothing lesser has been let feed on them. The glowing fungus grows where carcasses were boiled down for a long time.

**What the kill gives.** `heartroot` is a piece of the body that has not been boiled down. It still moves, as raw great remains do. It is the weakest of the eight remains and loosens only made things. That is the `tinker` bench, and the reason `tinker` comes. `apothecary` comes for the rest of the fresh remains. Boiled down, they are the first great lees a crew has made, and a hearth charged with it turns the whole woodland host. `mossheart_sprig` is a shoot off the body that still puts out leaf. Worn against the skin it does for the wearer what the salves cut from this animal do.

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step. While this animal lived the woodland kinds fed in the woods, on standing growth, where it allowed. With it dead their packs go for stores at dusk. This is `raid`, and it is in play.

## 4.4 `boss:2`: the burrower

**In the game.** `b_maw`, at `altar2` in `arena2`. A burrowing body of twelve segments. From the start it travels under a hunter and comes up beneath them, and it throws a fan of five balls of sand. At 45% four `dune_mite` come. After that it comes up with a ring of twelve balls of sand, throws two fans of seven, and two more `dune_mite` come. It drops `scorched_core`, sometimes `maw_fang`, salves and gold bars. The floor is a bowl of sandstone sunk three strides into the ground, with a rim of gilded brick, four pillars carrying lanterns, and the stone on a gilded platform. The bowls lie in every kind of country, sand or not.

**What it is.** The strongest of the sand kinds, which feed on stone. Where they feed in the open the stone falls to sand. Where they feed shut in under the deep rock it runs, and that is the molten rock (3.3). This animal travels between the two. It is bodied in grit, and its middle is bodied in rock from beside the molten rock. It has also fed on flesh. It took animals of the sand country, and it took people.

A feeder that passes deep under the ground crosses any ward (rule 6), so no charge turns this host from below. A great stone of this kind, held, turned everything weaker that walks. Against the sand kinds themselves there was one answer, which was to keep killing their strongest. To kill it the first comers had to bring it up. They dug a bowl and filled it with sand wherever they meant to do it, because loose sand is what it comes up through. Its own ground is the `desert`. The bowls elsewhere are places it was brought to.

**The bait.** `sunken_lure`: two blocks of sandstone, a gold bar, raw meat and `ember_dust`. `ember_dust` is the raw remains of `cinder_wisp`, a lesser animal of this host. The sandstone holds the remains and is the sand kind's own food. The gold is weight: it takes the lure down through loose sand, and it is the one metal a feeder gets nothing from (6.3). The meat is there because this animal eats flesh. A burrower finds the lure from below.

**The fight.** Travelling under the hunter is how it moves anywhere. The balls of sand are grit thrown off its body. At 45% it stays up and thrashes, and `dune_mite` come to the grit it sheds and to its wound.

**The floor.** The sand in the bowl was carried there. The rim and the platform are faced with gold because a stone-eating animal was going to lie against them, and gold gives it nothing to eat.

**What the kill gives.** `scorched_core` in a ring of deep rock is `forge2`. Householders say the core is hot enough to melt `cinder` ore. No fire melts it. The remains, woken by fire, loosen it. The same bed loosens `umbrite`. `maw_fang` is a tooth, the one part of the animal harder than its plates.

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step.

Nothing but its own host came up on the burrower's sand. That is the cause of the sand-country people, and the game does not yet show it. The change it needs: until `boss:2` is set, nothing hostile comes up on the sand of `desert` except `cinder_wisp`. A crew that crosses the sand before the kill can sleep on it. Once the flag is set, everything that comes up elsewhere comes up there too. The sand-country people lived on those terms and have lost them, and `ashen` follows.

The animal a crew first kills had taken sand-country people for as long as they had lived on its sand. They burn their own dead, as they burn every kill, so that nothing of theirs is left to draw (5.3). They could not burn what the burrower took, and they counted that its due. A crew boils the body down, and what was in it is now in a forge bed, in householders' pots and in coin. To the sand-country people that is the worst end there is: their dead, boiled and stored. It is the second reason the marches burn the crews' lees. *Proposed:* `deserter` has one plain line that says it, and no other text in the game does.

## 4.5 `boss:3`: the strongest of the shade-kind

**In the game.** `b_warden`, at `altar3` in `arena3`. It hangs in the air. From the start: two fans of three slow pale orbs, a change of place, a ring of twelve. At 60%: a turning spiral of three arms, two fans of five, a change of place. At 25% four `gloomshade` come: ten orbs on marked ground, a spiral of four arms, a change of place, a ring of eighteen. It drops `warden_sigil`, sometimes `wardens_aegis`, salves and lumenite. The floor is a pad of `wardstone` and stone brick with eight pillars of a pale stone capped with blue crystal.

**What it is.** The strongest of the shade-kind, which took its body in the hollow and went up into the deep rock of the living ground (3.4). The shade-kind comes to anything dead (7.3). Its strongest fed on something better. Its ground was the deep rock under the surface. The deep kind came up into that rock on every dark night, and this animal ate what came (1.6). It is pale with them. Lees set hard close to the wood is the gem `lumenite` (6.2), and an animal that has eaten the deep kind for that long is bodied partly in it.

The game draws it upright, with two upper limbs. It is no likeness of a person, and art should give it no face and no hands.

**The bait.** `warden_bell`: two iron bars, lumenite, two `shade_essence` and two bones. Essence and bone are the raw remains of `gloomshade`. The iron is the vessel, a cup cast with lumenite in its wall, and lumenite is this animal's own matter. Set mouth down on the stone, the cup holds the remains against the stone. The born ring it. The sound does nothing.

**The fight.** The orbs are lumps of the pale matter of its body. They are slow because they hang in the air as it does. What the game calls raising both hands is the animal turning to throw in spirals. At 25% it begins to fail to hold its body, and `gloomshade` come in for it.

**The floor.** A carcass boiled down leaves bone. The pillars are the bone of the shade-kind animals the first comers boiled down here, stacked. The blue caps are spilled lees, set.

**What the kill gives.** The remains of an animal bodied in the dark of the hollow hold a relation to the hollow. Seven pieces of `nightglass` round one `warden_sigil` is the first gate. The sigil is a piece of the animal. `beastcaller` comes to a crew that has made this kill, because that trade lives on shade remains and such a crew can supply them. `wardens_aegis` is a plate of the pale set matter of its body. It is dense, and a person carrying it is hard to knock off their feet.

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step. This animal ate the deep kind below on every dark night. From the night after the kill the deep kind comes all the way up. This is `moon`, and it is in play.

## 4.6 `boss:4`: the strongest of the hollow's fungus kinds

**In the game.** `b_sporemother`, at `altar4` in `arena4`, in the hollow. It hangs over its floor. From the start: eight spore bombs on marked ground, two `sporeling`, a ring of fourteen bombs. At 55%: a spiral of four arms, a ring of twenty, twelve bombs on marked ground, three `sporeling`. At 20%: a charge, a slam for eight strides with a ring of sixteen, a spiral of five arms. It drops `queen_spore`, sometimes `mycelial_crown`, salves and `umbrite` bars. The floor is a domed cavern of fungus turf with six giant fungus trees, four hanging fungus lamps, and the stone on a platform of `gloomstone`.

**What it is.** The strongest of the spore and fungus kinds of the hollow. It has fed longest on the fungus of the hollow, and that fungus grows on what dies there. It is bodied in cap and thread. It hangs from the roof of its cavern by its own growth, and the dome over the floor is that growth.

Pieces of cap fall from it all the time. They are raw feeder remains. The lowest feeders form on them, as one forms on a totem, and stay near it as a totem's animal stays near the hand. The born see small ones round a large one and say mother.

**The bait.** `spore_censer`: two `umbrite` bars, three `spore_sac` and a glowing fungus. The sacs are the raw remains of `sporeling`. The air of the hollow does not move, and a carcass on a stone does not carry there. The censer smoulders the sacs on the fungus, and the smoke carries. It is made of `umbrite` because raw sacs eat through iron.

**The fight.** Everything falls from above because the animal is overhead. The bombs are pieces of cap, and `sporeling` form where they land. At 55% the wound opens the cap, and the inside gives the light all fungus gives there. At 20% most of the growth that held it tears, and it swings on what is left. The charge and the slam are its own weight thrown about.

**The floor.** The first comers used the giant fungus trees as the posts of the floor. The hanging lamps are the lamps of that country.

**What the kill gives.** `queen_spore` in a ring of `gloomstone` is `forge3`, which loosens `noctrium` and `aether`. The item text says it feeds a kiln that burns without heat. That is what a hand feels: the remains do the work and the ore is never hot. `mycelial_crown` is a piece of the cap. Low feeders form on it as they formed on the pieces that fell from the animal, and a person who wears it can have one more of them beside the hand.

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step.

While it hung there the host stayed round it, since the host's food fell from it. No pack left the hollow. With it dead the packs go where food is. The stores are on the living ground, and the way there is the crew's own gate. A standing gate is a store of great remains with no hand on it, and it is an opening on the far place. *Proposed:* once `boss:4` is set, the hollow kinds of this host come up in the dark near any gate to the hollow that stands on the living ground. Pull one piece from the frame and the gate goes dark, as it does now. Before this kill a crew could leave its gate standing beside its houses. After it, a gate is shut behind the last one through or stands far from the beds.

The same holds for both gates: a far host's packs come up near a gate to their country once that host's strongest is dead.

## 4.7 `boss:5`: the flier

**In the game.** `b_regent`, at `altar5` in `arena5`, on the living ground. It flies, circling thirteen strides out and eight up, and does not land. From the start it stoops on a hunter and throws a fan of five bolts. At 60%: twelve strikes on marked ground, a ring of sixteen, a stoop. At 25%: a spiral of four arms, a stoop, sixteen strikes. It drops `storm_plume`, sometimes `tempest_feather`, salves and `noctrium` bars. The floor is a pad of stone brick and `storm_block` with six tall pillars of `storm_block`.

**What it is.** The strongest of the flying host. It took its body on the islands, where its kind feeds on the pale sheets that hang under the last ground (3.5). Those sheets are ground that was already part gone, and this kind eats what firmness is left in them. The animal is bodied in that pale stuff. Like every feeder it comes up anywhere on the root, and it comes up in the high air over the living ground.

A feeder that never lands crosses every ward (rule 6), and no hearth's edge has ever stopped this animal. A great stone of this kind, held, turned everything weaker that walks. The stone is first a killing floor for the one kind no ward turns.

**The bait.** `storm_kite`: two lengths of `weave`, three feathers, a `noctrium` bar and `moth_dust`. The dust is the raw remains of `umbral_moth`, a lesser flier of this host that lives in the hollow. The kite puts the remains in the air, which is the only place this animal feeds. The feathers are ordinary bird feather and make it fly. The bar holds the dust and steadies the cord. The kite is flown from the stone.

**The fight.** The stoop is how it takes anything. The bolts are pieces of its body, like every other throw (4.2). The game draws them as lightning and its lines speak of a storm, which is the born's word for the animal overhead. The strikes on marked ground fall from it because it is overhead.

**The floor.** The tall pillars are masts for kites. `storm_block` is stone its throws have loosened and that has set again as glass, on the pad and up the masts.

**What the kill gives.** The remains of an animal bodied in the stuff of the islands hold a relation to the islands. Six `noctrium` bars round one `storm_plume` is the second gate. The plume is a piece of the pale stuff, and it stands up from the hand however it is held. Arrivals see it point upward and take that as proof that the islands are in the sky overhead (1.8). `tempest_feather` is the same stuff. It bears weight and has almost none, and a person who wears it lands softly (6.7).

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step.

Its ground was the high air. The lesser fliers of the living ground stayed low, in the caves and under the crags where they roost. *Proposed:* once `boss:5` is set, `crag_bat` is in the air at night over every country. It roosts, so a house ward turns it like anything that walks. *Proposed* also: `rift_ray` comes up near any gate to the islands that stands on the living ground. It never lands, so no house ward turns it, and the rule that nothing comes up within 28 strides of an occupied home does not cover it. Every such gate is built from this animal's remains, so no crew has one before this kill.

## 4.8 `boss:6`: the strongest of the islands' crystal kinds

**In the game.** `b_luminarch`, at `altar6` in `arena6`, on the islands. It hangs, with a ring of crystals turning round it. From the start: a beam that turns slowly toward a hunter, three fans of four rays, a change of place. At 60%: a spiral of four arms, the beam, a ring of twenty, a change of place. At 25% two `shardling` come: sixteen strikes on marked ground, the beam, a spiral of six arms, a change of place. It drops `prism_heart`, sometimes `prism_core`, salves and `aether` bars. The floor is a disc of `riftstone` flecked with prism crystal, with eight pillars of the same crystal.

**What it is.** The strongest of the crystal kinds of the islands. Crystal is old lees set hard. The islands are what is left of a whole world. Feeders died there uneaten for as long as that world took to end, and what they left has set harder than anywhere else. The island kinds eat that crystal. This host feeds on the set remains of feeders, and its strongest is bodied in the densest crystal on the root.

**The bait.** `prism_lens`: two `aether` bars, three `shard` and a pane of glass. The shards are the raw remains of `shardling`. They cut and loosen whatever holds them. Glass in a rim of the islands' own metal holds them flat on the stone. The born say the lens gathers light and that the light calls the animal, which is their account of a bait that works by its remains.

**The fight.** A staff is crystal that a hand drives, and what it throws loosens whatever is set (1.6). This animal is crystal with a feeder in it. The rays and the beam are what a staff throws, at the size of its body, with no hand and no arm to tire. Crystal gives light, and the light does no harm. The ring round it is pieces of its own body. At 25% it stops holding its body. Pieces come away, and `shardling` form on them.

**The floor.** The flecks in the disc and the eight pillars are lees. The first comers boiled carcasses down on this floor, and on the islands what spills sets hardest.

**What the kill gives.** `prism_heart` in a ring of `riftstone` is `forge4`, which loosens `prism` ore. `prism_core` is a piece of the densest crystal there is. It is the best seat a cut line can run to, and a person who wears it drives with less loss (6.6).

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step. The island host stayed on the islands with its strongest. *Proposed:* with it dead its packs come up near any gate to the islands that stands on the living ground, by the rule given under `boss:4`.

## 4.9 `boss:7`: the strongest of the deep kind

**In the game.** `b_hart`, at `altar7` in `arena7`, on the living ground, at night only. It walks and gallops and carries antlers. From the start: a charge, a slam for seven strides with a ring of twelve blades, a fan of five blades. At 60% three `moonspawn` come: a change of place, a charge, a ring of twenty, twelve strikes on marked ground. At 25%: a charge, a change of place, a spiral of five arms, a slam for nine strides with a ring of eighteen. It drops `eclipse_ember`, sometimes `eclipse_shard`, `prism` bars and `moon_sliver`. The floor is a pad of `nightglass` and deep rock with ten pillars of `nightglass` capped with violet crystal.

**What it is.** The strongest of the deep kind, which lives in the deepest part of the root, bears no light and feeds on other feeders (1.6). Nobody can go where it lives. The first comers killed its like where it could be brought, on the surface, at night, with the dawn behind them. So its stones are on the living ground. It is drawn to them still, as each great feeder is drawn to the stones of its own kind. Moonlight on the open floor stops it coming up there, and on the dark night nothing does. Its host goes where it goes (1.6).

**Its shape.** It runs on four legs and carries tines. A feeder's shape belongs to its kind (7.1), and this is the shape of the deep kind's largest. Its body is lees set hard and pale. Lees that sets in the open grows outward as standing crystal does (6.3), and the tines are that: its own body grown out.

**The bait.** `eclipse_mask`: two `prism` bars, three `moon_sliver` and two `shade_essence`. The slivers are bits of the bodies of the deep kind, its own host's lesser animals. The essence is shade-kind, which is what the deep kind eats on the surface. The metal holds them. It is laid on the stone like every other bait. The born say it is worn and that the animal sees the wearer. Bait brings this animal up under any moon (1.6). Nothing brings it up under the sun.

**The fight.** It charges because it hunts by running. The blades are slivers of its body. The light on its antlers is the violet light its kind gives. At 60% it starts to change place, and `moonspawn` come in for it. The sun undoes its kind. The game does not harm it if a fight runs into morning, and it should go back down at first light (chapter 8, changes).

**The floor.** The pad and pillars are the black glass of the deepest rock. The violet caps are the lees of the deep kind, left by the dawn and set.

**What the kill gives.** `eclipse_ember` in a ring of `nightglass` is `forge5`, which loosens `eclipse` ore, the ore that lies on the wood. The item text calls the ember a coal from the last eclipse. It is a piece of remains that gives its kind's light. `eclipse_shard` is a sliver of the strongest low remains a person can carry. It loosens a little wherever the bearer strikes, and every kind of blow lands harder for it.

**What is lost.** The host breaks, and every hostile creature hardens by one step. On the dark night the deep kind came up as a host under a strongest, and other packs stayed off the ground. Leaderless, it comes up as packs, and packs with nothing over them do not hold ground. *Proposed:* once `boss:7` is set, `raid` can fall on the dark night.

## 4.10 `boss:8`: what lies in the base of the eighth stone

**In the game.** `b_sovereign`, at `altar8` in `arena8`. It is a stack of stone hanging in the air with a ring of standing stones on top. From the start: two fans of five lit stones, a slam for eight strides with a ring of fourteen, a ring of eighteen. At 66%: a spiral of five arms, a beam, sixteen strikes on marked ground, a change of place. At 33% three `moonspawn` come: a charge, a spiral of seven arms, the beam, twenty-two strikes, a change of place, a ring of twenty-six. It drops one `cairn_heart`, one `sovereign_light`, `eclipse` bars, and at even odds each of four arms found nowhere else. The floor is a pad of stone brick and cobble, eight heaps of stone topped with lanterns, and a raised base of `wardstone` with the stone on top. Stones of this kind lie the furthest apart of the eight.

**What it is.** The strongest of the stone-eating kind. That kind feeds on rock and coal everywhere under the ground, and it was here before anyone dressed a stone (7.3). It is the strongest of the eight kinds, and the first comers reached it last, with the last metal. Its stones stand furthest apart because its charge reached furthest. The housing is the largest they cut, and it stands on a raised base of dressed stone that held the store for its charge: sealed great lees, and the arms of the crews that worked that stone.

When the stones went dark, the strongest of each stone-eating host came to the stones of its own smell like every other. To this kind the base was food as well as carrion. It has eaten into the base from beneath and lies in it. It is bodied in dressed stone. It has eaten the lamps that were set in the housing and the store under it.

**What each side says.** Householders see a housing on a heap of stone ringed with lamps and call it a cairn. They say their forebears closed their first and best stone under it when the great lees ran out, to save it for the day it could be lit again. *First* in their mouths means chief: the stone that covered the most ground. The sand-country people say the heap was closed over a thing that could not be killed, by people who then set lamps round it.

Nothing was closed. The base was built as it stands. Each telling has a use. The first makes the base an act of care and the animal a store that can be opened. The second makes the eighth stone the proof that stones breed great feeders. The householders' telling has to leave out that a great stone is carrion when it is dark. The sand-country telling has to leave out that the like of this animal was killed, often enough for stones to be cut for its charge and spaced by its reach.

A player can see two things. The arms that come out of the animal are better than anything in any other store. And the base, once the animal has come up through it, is hollow.

**The bait.** `cairn_key`: three `eclipse` bars, a block of lumenite, two `moon_sliver` and two `shard`. It is a small housing with no holder. The lumenite is its stone, the metal binds it, and the raw slivers and shards are its charge: the remains of the two strongest low kinds a crew can carry. Laid on the great stone, it is what this animal eats: stone with a charge on it that nobody is driving. The animal comes up through the base to take it. The born say the key opens the cairn. The pattern is the first comers', like every other bait. It is made in `eclipse` metal because nothing softer holds that charge.

**The fight.** The lit stones it throws are the lamps of the housing, lumenite and crystal, which it ate. At 66% the stack parts and its lit middle shows, which the born call an eye. Its body is full of the cut stone and gem of housings. What a staff does with a hand on it, this does with none, and that is the beam. At 33% it is coming apart and everything in it shows at once. The `moonspawn` are the deep kind, leaderless by then, falling in behind the strongest thing present.

**What the kill gives.** The Guide gates nothing on it. `cairn_heart` is the raw remains of the strongest thing any living person has killed on this ground. Boiled down, one heart is one charge. The four arms are first comers' work from the store in the base. One of them is a totem whose head is a lamp from the housing (7.5). `sovereign_light` is a piece of housing with its gem in it. What it does for the bearer is the game's (1.9), and its item text is the born's belief.

**What its death does.**

- Every pack in the crew's country is leaderless, and there is nothing left to fall in behind. The last hardening step falls on every creature. This is in play.
- *Proposed:* the base stands broken at every `arena8` once `boss:8` is set, with one changed look for the block and the stone under it.
- *Proposed:* the stone-eating kind is in packs. They leave the old stores and go for laid stone with lees behind it, which is a hearth. `stonegnawer` comes up at night on the surface and comes with `raid`.
- No text may say that anything is lit. The banner, the last Guide step and its line state the householders' belief as fact (4.13). A stone is lit while a holder sits at it with a driven charge, for one night. The kill lights none.

**Living there afterwards.** The later systems start from these facts. None is in play.

All eight kinds are in packs. All eight kinds of stone answer bait. Every forge, gate and chest of remains draws, and there is more to be drawn.

One `cairn_heart` boiled down is one charge that turns everything left. Packed into a great housing and driven, it holds that housing's reach for one night. The cost is a ward's cost, unchanged. One person holds, with a hand on the stone until first light, and the arm pays. That person is a householder, since an arrival at the end of their strength is gone from the stone (1.6). No householder has gone to a great stone in any telling, and getting one to go is the crew's work (5.6). Others standing by add nothing to the holding. A held stone draws feeders as far as its edge, and the crew fights there. The charge is spent at dawn. A spent stone is carrion of the strongest thing ever boiled, and at the next dusk it draws from as far as it reached. So a stone held once has to be held again or left worse than it was found. Each night held costs one more killing.

Lesser great lees does the same against lesser things. The remains of `boss:1`, boiled down and packed into `altar1`, hold a small country against the woodland kinds and whatever is weaker. A crew can hold near ground cheaply and far ground dearly. That is the reason the first comers cut eight kinds of stone.

A host left alone gathers again. The animal that lies at a stone grows if nobody kills it, packs fall in behind it, and its ground closes to lesser things. A crew that stops hunting a kind gets that kind's old quiet back in time and loses its lees. *Proposed*, and dependent on all of the above: once `boss:8` is set, a pack gathers for some store at every dusk on which nobody holds a great stone.

This is the state in which the first comers held the country. Chapter 2 says how that ended.

## 4.11 What the peoples say the eight are

**Householders.** Each of the eight was set over a country by the forebears when the stones were raised, and has stood over it since. So they say warden and sovereign. The stone is an altar, where something is laid for the one set over the country, and the bait is an offering. Some householders still lay small kills at dark stones to keep the animal lying there (5.2). To kill it, in the telling `wayfinder` carries, is to take a country back, one step nearer the day every stone is lit. This account makes the animals part of the forebears' work, and it makes hunting them a repair. It leaves out that nobody set them, that each lies where the carrion is, and that each kill looses a host.

The account has a second use after each kill. When the deep kind first comes up, householders say the peopls broke a seal. When the base of the eighth stone stands broken, they say the peopls opened it. The Guide that sent the crew to both is their own telling.

Householders do not all hold it the same way. `wayfinder` tells it whole. Those who work remains speak of the eight as stock, by what each one's lees will turn. `beastcaller`, who feeds small feeders by hand, calls them beasts and is nearest the fact, and draws nothing from it about the stones. Some say the animal at a dark stone sits there only because nobody else does, and that it is the wrong holder. Some hold that the eight kinds of stone answer to the eight nights of the moon, and the item text on the remains of `boss:7` comes from them. The two have nothing to do with each other.

**The sand-country people.** The stones are what fattened the seven, and the eighth is the proof of it. They except the burrower. They say it let nothing else in, that it was the price of their country, and that it lay at no stone's bidding. It lay at `altar2`, in bowls the first comers dug.

**Arrivals.** The eight are the order of work, hardest prey last. Arrivals say of each kind that it comes back. Another animal answers. They take the last killing for the end of the work.

## 4.14 Open

- A standing gate is treated as a store that draws, like a forge or a chest of remains. No rule in play counts it yet.
- A fight in play ends when the one who laid the bait dies or goes more than 80 strides off. With several players it should end only when nobody is left on the floor (chapter 8, changes).
- Why a later animal of a kind fights at exactly the first one's strength. The cause given in 4.1 says it is as large. That the numbers are identical belongs to the game.
