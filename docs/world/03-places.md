# 3. The places

This chapter takes the ground country by country: the fourteen kinds of country on the living ground, the rock beneath them, the two kinds in the hollow and the two on the islands. For each it says how the land lies, why, what happened there and what is found there. It then goes through the thirteen kinds of old structure.

Section numbers beginning with 1 point to the ground chapter. Countries and structures are called by their internal keys until the naming pass. The name the game now shows for a country, in its debug overlay only, is given once in brackets. Distances are in strides, and a stride is one piece of ground.

Anything marked *proposed* is not in the generator yet. Chapter 8 lists all of it.

## 3.1 How the land lies

Three things made every country on this ground. Water and height gave it its first shape. The first comers cleared, built and held, and then stopped (chapter 2). The hosts have fed ever since, each kind on what it feeds on. Each country below is one mixture of the three.

**The same ground lies under every country.** Going down, the order is soil, stone, the darker deep rock (`deepstone`), and the wood. Ore lies by depth and by nothing else (1.6). No country is richer below than another, so there is nothing below for one hearth to hold against the next. What differs is how the depth is reached: by a ravine, by a cliff face in the high country, or by digging.

**All open water lies at one height.** The sea and the channels that run inland from it are one sheet, and nothing in it flows. Water is not set. It has run to the lowest ground it could reach and lies there. A village well is a roofed stone basin and does not reach it.

**Nothing comes up in water.** A low feeder takes its body from what it feeds on, and water has nothing set in it to take a body from. No feeder appears in the sea, in a channel or in a pool of the hollow. This holds in play today. Ice is ground and does not count as water.

**Heat and cold do not move.** There are no seasons, so no country warms or cools from one round of the moon to the next. Snow lies where it lies and has never been seen to melt. Height is cold: the pine wood whitens upward and the summits are all snow. Away from the heights, cold country and hot country lie in broad patches, and where they lie has no cause in the world (1.9).

**Nothing has fallen down.** A piece of ground stays where it is put, with or without anything under it. A wall on this ground does not lean, slump or lie in a heap. Where old work is missing pieces, a person broke them out or carried them off, or the stone-eating kind has been at them. Art for every old structure follows from this: whole pieces in their places, clean gaps where pieces are gone, and no rubble.

**Lit ground is any ground with light on it.** Feeders do not come up in lit places (1.6). In play that covers lamps and torches. It also covers, as far as their light reaches, the growth of the hollow, the leaves of the island trees and standing crystal. A country with its own light has standing places a dark country lacks. This matters most where the hour stands and no dawn comes.

**Nobody holds country.** No householder rules beyond a hearth, and nothing on this ground is a border. The only lines a walker crosses are the reach of the old great stones, which are dark, and the edge of the sand.

**The three places answer to each other stride for stride.** A gate raised at a spot on the living ground comes out at the matching spot in the hollow or among the islands, because the same wood lies under all three. Digging down does not reach the hollow and building up does not reach the islands. The match is along the wood. That is why arrivals speak of under the world and above it. In play today this is true of each player's first crossing only. After that any gate takes that player to the far mouth they first came out of, and two players can step through one gate and come out apart. The cause here asks for one matching spot for each gate, the same for everyone (chapter 8, changes).

## 3.2 The living ground

What the living ground is, its sky and its size are in 1.3. Two places on it are fixed and belong here.

**The mouth.** The dead road lies in the wood. The mouth is the place where it meets the root's upper face, and the living ground lies over that face like any other part of it. So there is no hole. The ground over the mouth is low, dry and open, and nothing is built on it or marks it. Someone who digs there comes to the floor, as anywhere. A person coming up the road is standing on that ground who was not there before, as a person coming through a gate is standing at its far side. A born person standing on it sees grass. Going down is a pathfinder's skill: knowing where a road lies in wood, and how to step into it. Nobody else can do it, and there is no pathfinder on the ground. The born do not sleep on that ground or dig in it, because of what comes up there.

**The nearest stone.** A stone of the first kind (`altar1`) stands within a few hundred strides of the mouth, most often to the south-east. Every other great stone lies where the spacing of its kind puts it. This one was set for the mouth, to cover the place where people and loads came out of the road (2.3).

Fourteen kinds of country follow. Three kinds of feeder are about in the green country by day: `thornling`, `gelcap` and, in the mire, `bog_lurker`. They are bodied in green growth (1.6). The cold country, the heights and the sand have little of that growth and none of the three, and by day nothing hostile is on their surface.

### The water

#### `ocean` (Open Sea)

**Lie.** The low ground under the one water. The floor is sand, with clay in the shallows and gravel in the deep. Where the country is cold the surface is ice.

**Cause.** Water ran to the lowest ground. Nothing comes up in it, so it is the one wide place on the living ground with no feeder in it at any hour.

**What happened.** *Proposed:* the first comers crossed it on foot. A piece set at the waterline stays there with nothing under it, so a crossing on this ground is a line of single dressed stones a stride apart. Such lines run out from some shores. A few reach the far side. Most stop in open water, at the stone where the work was when it stopped.

**Found.** `glimmerfin`, an ordinary fish. Clay for brick and for pots: every pot that holds lees is fired clay, since salt eats iron. Gravel, and flint in the gravel.

#### `beach` (Shore)

**Lie.** The low edge of the land. Sand, with three pieces of sandstone under it.

**Cause.** The sea works the edge of the land loose. Loose ground that lies long enough over the root sets again, and sandstone is sand that has.

**What happened.** Salt is made here. Every charge is packed in salt, and a store where the salt failed is a nest. The game has no salt. *Proposed:* square pans cut one piece deep into the shore sandstone, filled from the sea and left to dry in the sun. No fuel is used. Pans lie along every shore. Most are full of blown sand. A few near villages are clean, because a householder with a pot still needs salt.

**Found.** Sand, which fires to glass. Sandstone. Reeds where grass meets the water. No creature of its own. By day the open shore is safe walking, with nothing in the water on one side and no cover on the other.

### The green country

#### `plains` (Verdant Plains)

**Lie.** Dry open grass with a lone oak here and there.

**Cause.** Pasture. A hearth has to eat, and nothing is tilled on this ground (1.6). The `woolback` is the flock animal. The first comers cleared this country for flocks. The flocks have gone wild and still graze it, and their grazing is what holds it open.

**What happened.** This is the country given up last. Villages stand in it and householders on the road use them. *Proposed:* the corners of the stone folds where flocks were shut in at night, two short runs of loose stone meeting at a right angle with one oak left standing in the angle for shade. The rest of each wall was carried off for other work.

**Found.** `woolback` and `gobblet`. `gelcap` by day. Tall grass for fibre. Red and yellow flowers. Hide, which every `guest_bed` needs.

#### `palewood` (Palebark Wood)

**Lie.** A light wood of pale-barked trees (`pale_log`), with grass, flowers and berry bushes under them and a few oaks coming up between.

**Cause.** Pasture that was given up some generations back. The pale tree is the first to seed into open ground. Oak comes up under it later. The grass and flowers of the pasture are still on the floor.

**What happened.** Nothing that anyone decided. Hearths went dark one at a time, the flocks near them thinned, and trees came in. A walker can read how long a country has been left from its trees: open grass, then pale wood, then oak.

**Found.** Pale timber. Berries. `woolback` and `gobblet`. `thornling` by day.

#### `forest` (Greenwood)

**Lie.** Old oak wood, with fern, mushroom and berry under it and some pale trees through it.

**Cause.** Either this country was never cleared, or it was given up longest ago. The trees do not show which. Where a village stands under them, the ground was cleared and lived on.

**What happened.** The villages here stand whole under the trees. Through the unhunted generations the woodland kinds fed on standing growth, where their strongest allowed, and on ground that lies over the root a feeder's work on built timber is too slow to see in a lifetime.

**Found.** Oak, and apples from its leaves. Berries, `mushroom_brown`. `woolback`, `gobblet`, `burrowhog`. `thornling` and `gelcap` by day. The killing floor at every `altar1` is built of this country's stuff, oak posts and mossed stone, wherever it stands.

#### `bloomvale` (Bloomvale)

**Lie.** A rare country of short pale turf, thick flowers and blossoming trees (`rose_log`). Its white and blue flowers outnumber its red.

**Cause.** Growth from the islands. The white flower and the blue (`flower_white`, `flower_blue`) are the flowers of the islands (3.5), and the turf here is paler and bluer than the grass of the countries round it, toward the colour of island turf. Seed came through the first comers' island gates on feet and loads for as long as those gates stood. It took where the ground suited it and has gone on spreading by itself since. So this country marks where island growth took, and a gate need not have stood in any one stretch of it. The two flowers have also spread in patches through all the green country.

**What happened.** Nobody on the ground can read this country until someone has stood on the islands and come back. No born person has done that in any telling. A crew that returns from its first crossing has seen these flowers before.

**Found.** Blossom timber. All three flowers, the red among them, which the first bait uses. Villages. `woolback`. `thornling` and `gelcap` by day. Whether the blossom tree is an island tree changed by living ground, or a tree of the living ground changed by island seed, is not known. *Proposed:* one island tree (`star_log`, `star_leaves`) standing alone in such country. Its leaves give light, so nothing comes up under it.

#### `swamp` (Mirefen)

**Lie.** Wet lowland at the level of the one water. Mud, broad low oaks, reeds, mushrooms, and a haze that does not lift.

**Cause.** The ground here is barely above the water and never drains.

**What happened.** Nobody built here. Things that die in the mire go down into mud, where nothing renders them and little finds them, and lees that lies in mud sets as it does in rock.

**Found.** `bog_lurker`, here and nowhere else: the woodland kind grown old in mud. `thornling` and `gelcap`. `gobblet`. Reeds for fibre, `mushroom_brown` for the pot, and `bog_pearl`, a lump of lees set in mud, which the lurker swallows with the mud it lies in.

### The cold country

#### `taiga` (Frostpine Reach)

**Lie.** Cold pine wood with fern and berry under it. Snow lies on its higher ground.

**Cause.** Cold. The three kinds that are about in the green country by day are bodied in green growth, and the pine wood has too little of it. There are fewer mouths here than in the oak.

**What happened.** Villages of pine stand here, built and stocked like the rest.

**Found.** Pine. More berry bushes than any other country. `burrowhog` by day. `frost_stalker` in ones to threes at night.

#### `tundra` (Whitefell)

**Lie.** Flat and white. Snow on the grass, a pine or a dry bramble here and there, ice along its sea.

**Cause.** Cold. It has no timber to build with, no fuel and no flock, so no village was ever raised here. The great stones and the stores stand in it at their spacings all the same, since spacing took no account of country.

**What happened.** People crossed it to work the stones in it and went back.

**Found.** `burrowhog`. `frost_stalker` at night. By day nothing hostile walks it, and a stone or a store can be seen from a long way off. *Proposed:* a short run of prints that begins in the middle of clean snow, with no trail leading to it. A feeder is found where it was not (1.6). On every other ground that has to be taken on trust. On snow the prints show it.

### The hot country

#### `desert` (Amber Dunes)

**Lie.** Hot and dry. Sand, with six pieces of sandstone under it, `cactus` and dry bramble on it.

**Cause.** The sand kinds feed on stone (7.3). Where they feed in the open, the stone falls to grit, and sand is rock with the firmness eaten out of it. Where they feed shut in under the deep rock, the stone runs, and that is the molten rock (3.3). The sandstone lies twice as deep here as at any shore because this sand has lain longer and more of it has set again.

**What happened.** This was the burrower's ground, and the sand-country people lived on it (5.3). Nothing but the burrower's own host came up here while it lived. In play the shade-kind is on this sand from the first night, and that has to change for the cause to hold (chapter 8, changes).

The sand-country people burn every kill. A kill burns clean only in a hard fire, and where a hard fire has stood on sand for generations the sand under it has run to black glass, as sand does in a `forge1`. The sandstone villages round wells are first comers' villages like the rest.

*Proposed:* the place where the sand-country people live now. A floor of black glass ten or so strides across with one fire on it that burns day and night. Six to ten low sandstone houses stand in a ring round it with their doors to the fire. No pot is in any of them, and no chest. Fuel is stacked inside the ring. By day the people are at the fire and out on the sand. By night everyone is inside the light with a few awake at its edge, and `cinder_wisp` hang over the warm ash. One such place stands to a stretch of sand, about as far apart as villages. A march forms at the nearest one and sets out so as to arrive at first light.

**Found.** `dune_skipper`. `cinder_wisp` at night, whose `ember_dust` burns twice as long as coal. Sand, sandstone, `cactus`.

#### `savanna` (Amber Steppe)

**Lie.** Hot and half-watered. Tall dry grass with low flat-topped trees (`kapok_log`) far apart.

**Cause.** The tree is the tree of the wet wood (`jungle`). Here it stands thin because it has been cut for fuel for as long as fires have burned on the sand. The sand grows almost nothing that burns, and this is the nearest timber to it.

**What happened.** The sand-country people and the householders both use this country, and its villages show it. *Proposed:* in some of them every hearth floor is burned black. Householders say the sand-country people did it on a march. The telling that comes through `deserter` has it that the people of those houses did it themselves on the day they left. Nothing in the houses settles which (2.8).

**Found.** Villages. `woolback` and `dune_skipper`. `gelcap` by day. `cinder_wisp` at night. Dry bramble for sticks.

#### `jungle` (Verdance)

**Lie.** Hot wet wood. The tallest trees on the ground, to fifteen pieces, over fern.

**Cause.** Heat and water. It was never cleared, because no flock lives in it. The only ordinary animal here is the `gobblet`. A hearth could not be fed, so no village was built.

**What happened.** The great stones in this country were worked like any others, and whoever worked them had no house to go to. *Proposed:* they slept off the ground. A floor of planks set among the trunks stays where it is put. The steps were taken up after the last one in. The floors are still there, each with a lamp and a `crate`, and no way up.

**Found.** Tall timber. `gobblet`, whose feathers go into arrows and into the bait for the flier. `thornling`.

### The high country

#### `crags` (Highcrags)

**Lie.** High stone. Grass on the flats, bare rock on the slopes, a few pines.

**Cause.** Height. Hard rock is at the surface, and coal and iron show in the cliff faces.

**What happened.** Stone was cut here. A ward housing has to be hard stone, since soft stone loses its cut lines, and the first comers dressed a great deal of it. Ground parts along even joints and comes away in whole pieces, so a quarry on this ground is a squared bite in a slope. *Proposed:* such bites, with a block left half dressed at the foot. No village stands in the high country. It has no pasture and little level ground.

**Found.** `burrowhog`. `crag_bat` at night, which roosts under the overhangs. Coal and iron without digging.

#### `peaks` (Snowcrown Peaks)

**Lie.** Above the snow. Snow over stone, and nothing growing.

**Cause.** Height.

**What happened.** Arrivals say the islands are in the sky over the living ground (1.8). A column one piece wide stands for as long as the ground does, so an attempt to reach them is easy to make and stays where it was left. *Proposed:* columns of loose stone on some summits, far taller than any use would need, each ending in nothing. They are the work of arrivals who came up before the crew. Nobody born believes the islands are overhead, since no born person knows there are islands.

**Found.** `frost_stalker` and `crag_bat` at night. Coal in the faces.

## 3.3 Under the living ground

Caves are dark at all hours and feeders are up in them at all hours. Ravines open the deep rock to the sky in every country.

**The molten rock.** It lies in the lowest rock, just over the wood, under every country, and stands higher in some regions than in others. The root under it is cold. The molten rock is the sand kinds' feeding (see `desert`): rock with the firmness eaten out of it in a place where it cannot fall apart, so that it runs. It is hot because that kind's feeding is hot. `cinder_wisp` is found beside it, the same kind that comes up at night on the sand. Rock that ran and set again is the black glass (`nightglass`), and the iron that ran with it is `cinder` (6.2). In the regions where it stands highest the rock round it is `scoria`.

**Crystal caverns.** Azure and violet crystal stands on the floors and azure and amber hangs from the roofs. Crystal is old lees that lay uneaten (1.6). These caverns are in the rock that was the ground of the shade-kind's strongest, where it ate the deep kind as it came up on each dark night. What it did not finish lay where it fell. Violet is the lees of the deep kind and azure of the shade-kind's own dead. Nobody has matched amber to a kind.

**Fungal caverns.** The turf of the hollow (`mycel`) covers the floors, with the hollow's own glowing growth on it. This turf lies nowhere else on the living ground. It came through the first comers' gates to the hollow and took in the dark of the deep rock, as island growth took in `bloomvale`. *Proposed:* the same growth begins round any gate a crew leaves standing.

## 3.4 The hollow (`dim:1`)

**What it is.** The inside of the root: rock, standing water and fungus, with the wood for floor and roof. It is remains of an earlier world. Its rock (`gloomstone`) lies between two faces of the wood and is harder than anything in the deep rock of the living ground except the rock that lies on the wood itself. Wood of this kind does not rot or burn and nothing made here cuts it, so nothing on the ground accounts for a hollow in it. What hollowed the root is unknown to everyone, and no text explains it.

**Sky and edges.** It has no sky. From floor to roof is about a hundred and eighty pieces. The wood lies one piece under the deepest pool floors and one piece over the highest roofs, and a crew that digs there finds it black. Sideways no end has been found. Caverns open into caverns, and two kinds of country recur.

**The hour.** It does not move (1.3). With no sky there is nothing to show this. Nothing here is ever undone by a dawn, and feeders are up at all hours.

**Water and light.** Pools stand at one level in the lowest thirty pieces, and nothing comes up in them. The country gives its own light: fronds (`lumen_frond`), `glowshroom`, the lamps that hang from the roofs (`sporelamp`), and crystal. Lit ground is where a crew can stand. The banner on arrival says to bring light, and it is right about the stretches between.

**Why a gate reaches it.** The remains of `boss:3` hold the relation, because its kind took its body here (1.6). The frame is `nightglass`, from the deep rock of the living ground.

**What crossing costs.** The body is carried through whole and pays nothing. These are the costs in play today.

- A kill. The kit is built round great remains, and the first killing of `boss:3` is what lets the deep kind reach the surface.
- The bed. No bed marks anything on the far side. A fall there ends at the bed on the living ground, and the whole way has to be walked again.
- The nights. While a crew is below, its hearths are not supplied and its stores have nobody near them.

*Proposed,* two more. A gate is great remains with no hand on them, and it draws as a forge or a chest of them does, so packs come to where the near frame stands. And once the strongest of a far host is dead, that host's packs come up near any gate to their country that stands on the living ground (4.6).

**Who lives there.** Four kinds of feeder: `sporeling`, `mycelid_knight`, `umbral_moth`, `glowmaw`. No ordinary animal. No people: nobody can hold where there is no first light (1.6). The fungus needs no sun. It grows on what dies here, in a place where nothing is ever boiled down or burned.

**What the first comers left.** A stone to be baited (`altar4`) on its killing floor, and vaults (`cache1`). Nothing here was ever held. Nothing stands here from before them. *Proposed:* three `sporelamp` hung in a straight row under a roof. Growth scatters, so a straight row was hung by a person, and a row leads toward a stone or a vault.

#### `hollows` (Gloomroot Hollows)

**Lie.** Caverns floored with `mycel`, set with fronds, `glowshroom` and `mushroom_brown`, and with great capped fungi (`gloom_stem`) where the roof is high enough.

**Cause.** Fungus on everything that has died here.

**What happened.** The first comers mined it for `umbrite` and `noctrium` and baited its strongest. Then nothing, for the whole of the unhunted generations.

**Found.** `umbrite_ore` through all of it and `noctrium_ore` in the lower half. Coal and geodes. All four kinds of the hollow. `gloom_stem` is the only timber.

#### `hollows_crystal` (Glimmer Vaults)

**Lie.** Bare `gloomstone` floors with spires of azure and violet crystal, some taller than a person. Almost no fungus.

**Cause.** The same as the crystal caverns under the living ground (3.3), and older. The shade-kind took its body in the hollow, and this country was its ground before it went up into the deep rock of the living ground. Its strongest ate the deep kind here, and what it did not finish has stood setting ever since. The azure is the shade-kind's own dead. None of that kind is left in the hollow.

**Found.** Crystal standing in the open. `noctrium_ore` below. The four kinds of the hollow cross it, and it has none of its own.

## 3.5 The islands (`dim:2`)

**What they are.** What is left of the world that grew on the root while the root lived. They are remains of the same kind as the hollow. They are the oldest matter on the root, and their metals are harder than the hollow's. Set ground stays where it is, so the pieces that did not go are where they were, in two layers one above the other, with nothing between and nothing under.

**The sky.** It is that world's own sky, stopped at one dusk. The sun is just under the edge. The stars are out and do not turn. The bands of light low in the sky are the light of the standing crystal (see `rift_shards`), caught in the pale sheets that hang under the islands. It is no part of the living ground's sky, and no height of building on the living ground reaches it. A still sky does not show whether it is an evening or a morning. Some arrivals take it for a dawn and wait for the sun.

**Edges.** Every island has an edge and nothing below it. A fall does not land: the faller is back on the last footing, hurt by a third of their strength (1.3). So nobody has seen what is under the islands. Sideways the islands recur in both layers as far as anyone has gone.

**The ground part-gone.** Below the lower layer hangs a pale stuff (`cloud`) in sheets. It bears weight and does not move. It is ground that was going when the sky stopped, and stopped with it.

**Why a gate reaches them.** The remains of `boss:5` hold the relation, because that animal is bodied in the pale sheets (4.7). The frame is `noctrium`, a metal of the hollow, so the second gate cannot be made by a crew that has not used the first. Where the matching spot is open air, the far frame comes out on a foot of `riftstone` and the crew builds outward from it.

**What crossing costs.** Everything that crossing into the hollow costs (3.4). Also the edges. And the way between islands has to be laid a piece at a time, as the first comers laid theirs.

**Who lives there.** Three kinds of feeder: `rift_ray` and `aether_wisp` in the air, `shardling` on the ground. No ordinary animal. No people, for the same cause as in the hollow. Whether that world had people is unknown, and nothing of theirs has been found.

**What the first comers left.** A killing floor that is itself a made island (`arena6`), and small stores hung between the two layers (`cache2`). Nothing here was ever held.

#### `rift` (Skyrift Meadow)

**Lie.** Turf (`skyturf`) over soil over `riftstone`. Trees with lit leaves (`star_log`), grass, and the white and blue flowers.

**Cause.** This is the surface of the earlier world as it stood at its last dusk. Nothing here has moved through a day since.

**What happened.** The first comers camped under the trees, because the leaves give light and nothing comes up beneath them. They took the timber, and their feet carried the seed home (see `bloomvale`).

**Found.** `aetherium_ore`. Island timber. `skyfruit`, the fruit that hung on these trees at that dusk (6.9). `rift_ray` and `aether_wisp` overhead.

#### `rift_shards` (Prism Shoals)

**Lie.** Islands set with spires of pale crystal (`prism_crystal`), with white flowers between and no trees.

**Cause.** The oldest lees on the root. Whatever fed on that world and died on it uneaten left its lees on the surface, and it has had longer to set than any other crystal there is. The kinds of the islands are bodied in splintered crystal: they feed on it. This country is their pasture, and some spires are eaten down to stumps.

**What happened.** The first comers took crystal here and set their killing floor in it. The pillars round `altar6` are this crystal.

**Found.** `prismite_ore`. Standing crystal. `shardling`.

## 3.6 Places nobody is from

Nobody is from the hollow or the islands, and no telling was made in either. Householders call the hollow under and say what comes up at night comes from it, which is nearly true, and none of them has seen it. They have no word for the islands at all.

A builder can use this. In the hollow and on the islands nobody can say that the crew is trespassing, and nobody can be supplied, housed or wronged. The costs of working there fall on the living ground (3.4).

## 3.7 The thirteen kinds of structure

All thirteen are old. Nothing has been built on this ground since the hunting stopped, until the crew. Twelve are first comers' work. The thirteenth, the surface store, is the work of the born hunters who came just after them (2.4).

**`village`.** Four to six houses round a well, with packed paths from the well to each door, on flat dry ground in seven kinds of country: `plains`, `forest`, `palewood`, `bloomvale`, `taiga`, `savanna`, `desert`. Built in the first comers' time, of what the country has: plank and thatch, pine, or sandstone. Every door faces the well. One lamp hangs at the well, and it lights the ground between the doors, so a village is a ring of doors round one lit place. Each house has a `crate`, a lamp, and a `bedroll` or a `workbench`. State: whole and empty, with the crates stocked by householders who pass. The game shows no householder on the road. *Proposed:* one met in a village at dusk, sitting by a pot.

**`ruin`.** A surface store. A floor nine strides square with walls up to four pieces high, a lamp on a pillar in the middle, and a chest. It has no dressed stone of the first comers' make in it. State: broken into. The walls have clean gaps (3.1). The chest still holds something. Under half have a nest of the shade-kind. They stand in every dry country, settled or not.

**`dungeon`.** A sealed store. Three to five rooms of `wardstone` joined by passages, cut about where stone turns to deep rock, with no way in from above. Pillars carry lamps or azure crystal. Built as working stores for the crews that hunted at the stones. State: sealed and full. Salt has failed in most rooms and there are nests in them, `stonegnawer` and `rustbound` by turns. The last room holds the chest.

**The eight killing floors.** Each is a paved round with a ring of pillars and a great housing on a low dais at the centre. Each is whole. The animal that lies at a spent stone lets nothing lesser feed on its ground, so nothing has been at these floors in the whole of the unhunted generations. Chapter 4 says why each is built as it is.

On the living ground the stones lie further apart as the host is stronger, as reach would fall. The two that are not on the living ground lie closer than any above. Nobody can hold where the hour stands, so their spacing was set for the hunter's walk and for nothing else.

**`cache1`.** A vault in the hollow: one room of `wardstone` seven strides square, with a doorway one piece wide, two lamps of the country, a chest, and a nest of `mycelid_knight`. State: open at the doorway, salt failed.

**`cache2`.** A store on the islands: a disc of turf and `riftstone` nine strides across, hung between the two layers, with a chest and two posts of island timber capped with crystal. An island has no depth of rock to seal a room under, so this store is sealed by air. Nothing that walks can reach it. State: whole, and it has no nest.

## 3.8 What moves between countries

No country here lives on its own. What moves, and why:

- **Lees.** From every kill, in every country, to every pot. The only thing all hearths want every night.
- **Salt.** From the shore to wherever lees is packed.
- **Hide.** From pasture and from the cold wood to every bed.
- **Clay.** From the shallows, for pots and brick.
- **Hard stone.** From the heights. Nobody cuts it now. The first comers' dressed stone is what there is.
- **Timber and coal.** From wooded country to the sand, which has neither and burns more than any other country.

Nobody taxes any of it and nobody holds a source. Householders carry goods by day between the old houses. The one thing that cannot be carried from country to country by a born person is great lees, because getting it kills them.
